# AI tropes to remove

Readers spot these and stop trusting the page. Each one usually stands in for a fact that's missing. The fix is almost always to name the specific thing, not to find a synonym.

## Words and phrases

Cut these, or replace them with the plain word in parentheses.

- **Inflated verbs:** leverage (use), utilize (use), delve, elevate, unlock, empower, foster, streamline, revolutionize, transform, spearhead, harness, navigate (handle, get through), ensure (make sure, or just state the result)
- **Empty adjectives:** seamless, robust, comprehensive, holistic, innovative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, best-in-class, top-notch, unparalleled, second to none, meticulous, pivotal, crucial, vital, invaluable, bespoke, tailored, dynamic, vibrant
- **Filler nouns:** solutions, landscape (unless it means plantings), realm, journey, tapestry, testament, synergy, excellence, peace of mind, game-changer, vision (unless it's the owner's actual plan)
- **Vague quantities:** a wide range of, a variety of, numerous, countless, myriad, plethora, various. Give the number or the list.
- **Stock openers:** "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to", "In the world of", "It's important to note", "Look no further", "Rest assured", "Imagine..."
- **Stock closers:** "In conclusion", "Ultimately,", "At the end of the day", "That's the PH&C difference", "Your project is in good hands", "every step of the way", "from concept to completion", "from start to finish", "from the ground up" (unless literal)
- **Puffery:** "trusted partner", "one-stop shop", "dedicated to", "committed to excellence", "passionate about", "at the heart of", "plays a key/crucial/vital role", "bring your vision to life", "exactly as intended"
- **Place clichés:** nestled, boasts, "in the heart of"

## Sentence patterns

These are harder to catch than words and matter more.

1. **Contrast setup.** "Not just X, but Y." "X isn't about A. It's about B." "We treat pre-construction not as paperwork, but as..." Say Y directly.
2. **Reflexive threes.** "On time, on budget, and built to the standard you expect." "Transparency, communication, and clarity." Lists of three abstractions are the strongest AI tell. Use two concrete items, or four, or one.
3. **The participle tail.** "..., ensuring a smooth closeout." "..., allowing owners to save time." "..., keeping the project moving." The tail claims a benefit nobody demonstrated. End the sentence at the fact, or make the benefit its own sentence with evidence.
4. **The moral at the end.** Paragraphs that close with a summary line restating the point ("A well-developed program is the foundation that keeps everyone aligned"). Delete the last sentence and see if anything was lost. Usually nothing was.
5. **Generic first sentence.** "Great projects begin with a clearly defined program." "A successful project begins long before ground is broken." Opening with a truism delays the content. Start with what PH&C does.
6. **Rhetorical questions.** "Are you ready to put your project in good hands?" "What makes a project succeed?" State the answer instead.
7. **Colon and question reveals.** "The result? A site delivered early." "Here's the thing:" Just say it.
8. **Dramatic fragments.** "Every permit. Every inspection. Every time." Write full sentences.
9. **"Whether you're A or B" openers.** "Whether you need a single project or a full program..." Say who it's for in one plain sentence, or skip it.
10. **"From X to Y" sweeps.** "From early site evaluation through final closeout..." Name the two or three steps that matter.
11. **Em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or parentheses. One per page at most. Also avoid ` - ` as a dash.
12. **Bold-label bullets.** "**Expertise:** Our team brings..." on every bullet. Fine for a spec list, but not as a way to make marketing claims look organized.
13. **Uniform rhythm.** Every paragraph three sentences, every sentence 18 to 22 words. Real writing varies. A short sentence after a long one is fine. So is a two-sentence paragraph.
14. **Hedged absolutes.** "We anticipate every issue." "Every decision has been vetted." Nobody believes "every." Say what PH&C checks and when.
15. **Anthropomorphized things.** "The site tells a story." "Data drives decisions." People do things. Name them.
16. **Exclamation points and emoji.** None.

## Manual pass

After the checker runs clean, reread the draft and ask:

- **Could a competitor paste this on their site unchanged?** If yes, it has no PH&C facts in it. Add a project, a place, a permit, a person, or a number, or cut it.
- **Does every paragraph contain at least one noun you could photograph or look up?** A township, an agency, a tenant, a utility, an acreage.
- **Read it out loud.** Anything you wouldn't say to a client in a meeting goes.
- **Cut the first and last sentence of each paragraph** and see whether it got better. It usually does.
- **Count claims vs. evidence.** "We save owners money" needs a how: separate site contracts, bid comparisons, value-engineering items.
