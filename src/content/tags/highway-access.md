Before an owner prices a pad or a building, someone has to determine how vehicles will enter and leave the site. PH&C does that during feasibility. We identify who controls the road, which permits apply, and what improvements the agency is likely to require.

## What PH&C handles

- Determining whether the frontage is a state highway, a county road, or a local street.
- Identifying the permit path, such as a PennDOT highway occupancy permit, an NJDOT access permit, or a municipal driveway permit.
- Coordinating the traffic engineer on trip generation and access design.
- Meeting with agency staff before the plan is final.
- Estimating turn lanes, signals, widening, and drainage work.
- Testing the site layout against truck movements, queues, and circulation.
- Carrying the findings into the budget and the schedule.

## What you get

- A short memo covering the permit path, the likely improvements, and a cost range for off-site work.
- An early read on whether the site can be accessed the way the plan assumes.
- A budget that includes road improvements, which are often among the largest site costs.
- A basis for deciding whether to buy the property.

## How it connects

Once the project proceeds, the permits are managed under [highway permitting](/services/highway-permitting). Access is one part of [site analysis and feasibility](/services/site-analysis-feasibility).
