The project schedule is the one you use to make promises to a lender, a tenant, or a board. PH&C builds it before construction begins and keeps it current through design and permitting, so those promises rest on real activities with real durations.

## What goes into the schedule

- Design milestones for each phase, including owner review time.
- Land development, zoning, highway, stormwater, environmental, and utility approvals, each with an agency, a submission date, and a review period.
- Procurement steps, from bid documents through contract signing.
- Long-lead items such as utility service, specialty equipment, and structural materials.
- Construction sequencing, inspections, and turnover.
- Lender, grant, and tenant milestones that tie to dates in your agreements.

## How we use it

We identify the critical path, the chain of activities that sets the finish date, and we show the float on everything else. When a review runs long or a design decision slips, the update shows which later activities move and by how many days. You see the effect before it becomes a surprise.

## What you get

- A schedule that names the approvals and dependencies most likely to cause delay.
- Updates that explain changes in plain language.
- A baseline that carries into construction and is used for [schedule management](/services/project-schedule-management).
- A realistic basis for lease commencement dates, financing draws, and opening plans.
