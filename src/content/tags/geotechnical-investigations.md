What lies beneath a site is often the single largest source of hidden risk in a development budget. Before a shovel ever hits the ground, PH\&C leads a thorough geotechnical investigation to understand the soils, rock, and groundwater conditions that will ultimately govern how a project is designed, built, and priced. Getting this right early is one of the most effective ways we protect our clients from costly surprises during construction.

## Why geotechnical work matters

Every structure, pavement, and utility ultimately transfers its load into the ground. If the subsurface conditions are weaker, wetter, or more variable than assumed, the consequences show up as change orders, redesigns, and schedule delays at the worst possible time - after the contractor has mobilized. A well-scoped geotechnical program converts these unknowns into quantified, manageable design parameters. It answers the questions that drive real cost: How deep is competent bearing material? Is rock present, and will it require blasting? Where is the water table, and how will it affect excavation and foundations? Are the on-site soils suitable for reuse as structural fill, or will we be importing and exporting material by the truckload?

## What the investigation involves

PH\&C coordinates a licensed geotechnical engineer to plan and execute a program scaled to the project's size, structure type, and risk profile. A typical investigation includes:

* Soil borings and test pits positioned strategically across the building footprint, pavement areas, and utility corridors.
* Standard Penetration Testing (SPT) and, where warranted, cone penetration testing to characterize soil strength and density.
* Laboratory testing of recovered samples for classification, moisture content, compaction characteristics, and corrosivity.
* Groundwater monitoring to establish seasonal high water levels.
* Screening for problem conditions such as expansive or compressible soils, undocumented fill, organics, sinkholes and karst features, or contamination that overlaps with environmental due diligence.

The deliverable is a geotechnical report that provides allowable bearing pressures, recommended foundation types, slab and pavement subgrade preparation, lateral earth pressures for retaining walls, seismic site class, and guidance on excavation, dewatering, and earthwork. On complex sites we will also recommend supplemental studies - infiltration testing for stormwater management, additional deep borings for potential pile or pier foundations, or geophysical surveys where karst or voids are suspected - so the design team is never working from assumptions where the ground demands certainty.

## How PH\&C adds value

We do more than commission a report and file it. As your project manager, PH\&C makes sure the investigation is scoped to answer the questions that actually move the budget, that boring locations reflect the real building program, and that the findings are translated into design and cost decisions rather than sitting on a shelf. We fold the recommendations into the structural and civil design, the earthwork budget, and the construction schedule, and we flag any condition - deep foundations, over-excavation, undercutting, dewatering, or import fill - that carries meaningful cost so ownership can make informed decisions before committing funds.

Ordering geotechnical work at the feasibility stage, rather than after design is complete, gives our clients the leverage to adjust building placement, foundation strategy, or even the decision to pursue a site at all. That early insight is exactly what allows PH\&C to keep projects moving forward on solid ground - literally and financially.
