A budget says what the project costs. A cash flow says which month the money is needed. PH&C builds that forecast from the schedule and the contracts, instead of spreading the total evenly across the construction period.

## What PH&C handles

- Mapping each cost to the month it will be paid: land, design, permits, deposits, site work, building, and closeout.
- Soft-cost timing for fees, utility deposits, insurance, financing costs, and legal.
- Retainage and the dates it is released.
- Equity and loan draw schedules that follow the forecast.
- Peak funding months and the periods with little spending.
- Updates when a change order, a delayed approval, or a revised schedule moves the curve.

## What you get

- A month-by-month forecast that lenders and equity partners can review.
- Early warning of a funding gap or an idle balance.
- A tool for planning interest reserves and carry costs.
- Clear identification of which source of funds pays for which invoice.

## How it connects

The cash flow starts with the [pro forma and budget](/services/proforma-analysis-budgeting) and the [project schedule](/services/project-schedule). During construction, it is updated alongside [contract and budget management](/services/contract-budget-management) and [bank billing coordination](/services/bank-billing-inspection-coordination). If you have a schedule and a list of funding sources, [contact PH&C](/contact) and we will mark the months that need cash on hand.
