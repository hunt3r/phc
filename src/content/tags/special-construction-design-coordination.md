Some parts of a building come from specialists rather than the architect's standard drawings. Refrigeration, process equipment, medical systems, specialty structures, and operator or tenant prototypes all arrive with their own engineers and their own requirements. PH&C coordinates that work with the base building and the site before the field finds the conflict.

## What PH&C handles

- Identifying which systems and requirements need a specialty consultant.
- Bringing those consultants into the design process at the right time.
- Coordinating loads, clearances, structure, power, plumbing, and ventilation between specialty and base-building designers.
- Confirming who furnishes, who installs, and who connects each piece of equipment.
- Checking that specialty requirements are reflected in the site plan, utilities, and budget.
- Tracking submittals and shop drawings against the design.

## What you get

- A coordination set that overlays specialty drawings on the architectural and engineering backgrounds.
- A responsibility matrix for equipment, installation, and connections.
- Conflicts resolved on paper, where changes cost the least.
- Fewer field changes and fewer disputes over scope.
