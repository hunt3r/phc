Earthwork on a development site waits on its stormwater permit. PH&C manages the NPDES process for the owner. The civil engineer designs the erosion and sediment controls and the post-construction stormwater system. We coordinate the submission to the county conservation district and the Department of Environmental Protection and keep it on schedule with the other approvals.

## What PH&C handles

- Confirming whether the planned disturbance requires an NPDES construction stormwater permit.
- Coordinating the civil engineer's erosion and sediment control plan and stormwater management design.
- Preparing and submitting the application, and tracking review.
- Answering technical comments with the design team, so one response closes each item.
- Keeping the permit drawings consistent with the land development plan.
- Budgeting permit fees, basins, and erosion controls.
- Scheduling inspections and maintaining records during construction.

## What you get

- A permit schedule aligned with municipal approvals and contractor mobilization.
- Stormwater costs identified before bidding.
- One set of drawings that the township, the conservation district, and the contractor can all use.
- Fewer stop-work risks from missing or misplaced erosion controls.

## How it connects

NPDES permitting runs beside [land development and zoning approvals](/services/land-development-zoning-approvals) and [environmental permitting](/services/environmental-permitting). Early on, [environmental permitting requirements](/services/environmental-permitting-requirements) identifies when a permit is needed. If your project will disturb more than a small area, [contact PH&C](/contact) and we will map the schedule.
