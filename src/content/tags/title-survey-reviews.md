Title shows what the recorded documents say about a property. A survey shows what is on the ground. PH&C reads them together, because the problems appear where the two disagree.

## What PH&C handles

- Reviewing the title commitment and every listed exception.
- Obtaining and reading the recorded easements, restrictions, and agreements.
- Checking the boundary, topographic, or ALTA/NSPS survey against the legal description and the title exceptions.
- Plotting easements, rights-of-way, setbacks, and encroachments on the site plan.
- Identifying conditions that limit buildable area, access, or utility routing.
- Coordinating the surveyor, the title company, and your attorney.
- Requesting survey corrections and title clarifications before closing.

## What you get

- A marked-up exhibit that shows what you cannot build on and why.
- A list of exceptions that still need action, such as a release, a relocation, or an agreement.
- Information the civil engineer and architect can use from the first sketch.
- Protection against redesign after a recorded easement or boundary issue turns up late.
