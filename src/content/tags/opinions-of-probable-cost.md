An opinion of probable cost is an early budget that states its assumptions. PH&C prepares it during feasibility, when the drawings are a sketch or a program, so you can decide whether the next dollar should be spent.

## What PH&C handles

- Separating hard costs (site work, building, tenant work) from soft costs (design, permits, legal, insurance, financing, commissions).
- Pricing the drivers visible at this stage: earthwork, retaining walls, utilities, off-site road work, and remediation.
- Using current regional unit prices and noting where a price is a placeholder.
- Sizing contingency to the level of information available.
- Comparing the total against your pro forma and financing assumptions.
- Showing which line items would have to change for the deal to work.
- Updating the opinion as studies and drawings come in.

## What you get

- A budget you can take to a lender or an investor.
- Visibility into the items that drive cost, rather than a single total.
- Alternatives when the numbers do not work, such as a smaller building, a different site, or a phased scope.
- A foundation for the detailed estimate that follows.
