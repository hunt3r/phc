An RFP is the document bidders price. If it is vague, the low number is missing scope. PH&C writes the request so each contractor prices the same drawings, the same alternates, and the same contract.

## What goes into the RFP

- Drawings and specifications, with the bid set identified by date and revision.
- A bid form with line items that match the estimate, so the comparison is straightforward.
- Allowances and unit prices for conditions that cannot be known in advance, such as rock or unsuitable soils.
- The milestone schedule, working hours, and site rules, including rules for occupied properties.
- Insurance, bonding, and licensing requirements.
- The agreement the owner expects to sign, with any special conditions.
- Evaluation criteria: price, schedule, relevant experience, and the staff named in the proposal.
- Instructions for questions, addenda, and the bid date.

## What we manage during bidding

- Pre-bid meetings and site walks so every bidder hears the same answers.
- Written addenda issued to all bidders with time to reprice.
- Bid opening and the initial review for completeness.

## What you get

- Bids that can be compared line by line.
- Fewer exclusions and qualifications hidden in the fine print.
- A defensible record of how the contractor was chosen.
